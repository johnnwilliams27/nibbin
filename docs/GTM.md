## 1. Positioning

**Category:** AI agents for people who work for themselves — and for the organizations they grow into.
**Against beige AI:** every competitor sells "automation" in gray dashboards. Nibbin sells
*employees you can see growing* — agents with report cards. The creature isn't decoration;
it's the legibility of trust, and it's the most screenshot-able object in the category.
**Against fear (the real enemy):** solo owners are afraid of AI embarrassing them in front
of clients and afraid of surveillance. Both answers are architectural and we say them plainly:
*nothing sends without your approval — you grant what each Nibbin may do* (action level), and *screen
captures never leave your device* (Observer). Privacy is a growth asset, not fine print.
**Hero/tagline (locked):** "Nibbin is the AI team that learns how you operate — and runs your day so you don't have to."
**Pitch opener (locked):** "Every other AI makes you explain how you work. Nibbin learns on the job."
**Spectrum line (locked):** "Simple enough for a layman, extensible to a full enterprise — the same brain either way."
**Category line:** "AI Agents. Simplified." — used in SEO titles, meta,
Product Hunt tagline, directories, and the landing eyebrow. Deliberately
audience-agnostic: the public label names *what* we do, not *who* it's for, so solo
owners, entrepreneurs, and organizations self-ID. Human-voice copy keeps
"people who work for themselves" for the solo-owner register; the spectrum line ("same brain either way") is the bridge to organizational buyers. (Founder decision 2026-06-15:
widened the public label from "AI agents for freelancers" so the copy no longer
prescribes the target audience; the wedge sequence below still governs who we serve first. 2026-06-22: broadened further to one-person-or-org per Company Brain repositioning D19.)
**Proof number:** first agent in 10 minutes (TTFAD is a marketing SLO, per SPEC §6.12).

**Wedge:** solo photographers (weddings/portraits/events). Why: acute admin pain (inquiries,
galleries, invoices, mini-session logistics), dense communities, educator-driven trust graph,
portal-heavy tools that APIs don't reach (the Observer's moat), willingness to pay for time.
Expansion verticals (same playbook, later): designers/creatives, coaches/consultants,
tradespeople, Etsy sellers.

## 2. Phased plan

### Phase 0 — Build in public (now → M7, "the hatching")
- **Founding story content:** John runs user-zero — his own 14-day study and diagnosis is
  episode one. Document the build (the creatures, the trust model, the privacy architecture)
  on X/LinkedIn + a launch blog. Audience: AI-curious builders amplify; photographers convert later.
- **Waitlist:** nibbin.com gains an email capture ("Adopt your first Nibbin — join the grove").
  Position the Google 100-user cap as a feature: **the Founding Grove, 100 seats.** Scarcity
  that's actually true.
- **Seed the photographer beachhead:** join 10 photographer FB groups + 3 subreddits as a
  genuine participant (Scout agent surfaces threads; John posts as himself). Zero pitching
  for 60 days — build credibility first.
- **Educator pipeline opens:** Quill agent builds the list (podcasts, YouTube educators,
  workshop leaders); first 10 relationships warmed before there's anything to sell.

### Phase 1 — Founding Grove beta (M7 → public launch)
- 100 founding members, photographer-weighted, white-glove onboarded (the $200 guided
  session is free for founders — it's user research).
- **Deliverable per founder:** a diagnosis with real numbers. Target: 10 written case studies
  ("Maya got 6.2 hrs/week back; here's the map"). Numbers are the entire ad campaign.
- Referral mechanics live: each founder can gift 3 grove invites (skip-the-waitlist), invites
  carry the founder's Nibbin on the card.
- Educator partnerships convert: 3–5 educators run it free, affiliate terms ready (30%
  recurring, 12 months) for launch.
- Pricing validation, churn interviews, TTFAD tuning. Exit criteria: TTFAD median <10 min, 
  W4 graduate rate >25%, 10 case studies — at least 5 with photo + quote + website rights secured, 
  CASA verification passed (lifts the 100 cap).

### Phase 2 — Public launch
- **Launch week stack:** Product Hunt (creature brand is built for it) + Hacker News
  (privacy-architecture post: "We built screen observation that can't phone home") + the
  case-study drop + educator affiliate posts, same week.
- **The diagnosis share loop** ships in product: every diagnosis renders a beautiful share
  card (hours found, top three time sinks, your grove) — opt-in, watermarked nibbin.com.
  Graduations generate share cards too ("Penny graduated — 247 invoices, 98.4% accuracy").
- Content engine to full cadence (§4). Paid stays off except $500/mo of search on
  high-intent terms ("honeybook automation", "photography admin help") to harvest demand.

### Phase 3 — Vertical expansion (post-PMF signal)
- Repeat per vertical: 90 days of community presence → 10 case studies → educator/affiliate
  ring → vertical landing page + connector pack. The playbook is the product.

## 4. Content engine (the cadence)

- 3 short videos/week (creature moments, before/after time maps, 30-second "what my agents
  did this week").
- 1 long piece/week alternating: SEO article ↔ build-in-public post.
- 1 newsletter/2 weeks: "Field Notes from the Grove" — product progress + one founder story.
- Community: 5 genuine answers/week minimum (answers, not pitches).
- Everything drafted by agents, **everything public approved by John** — the company runs
  its own Agent School (§6).

### 4.5 Social proof on the website

- **Testimonial band on the landing page** ships at Phase 2 launch: real founder name,
  real photo, real number ("6.2 hrs/week back"), linked to the full case study. For
  photographers, use their own photography as the visual (with rights) — authentic and
  beautiful beats stock by a mile.
- **Collection is built into the beta, not asked for later:** the photo + quote + website
  rights ask happens at the two peak-delight moments — the Diagnosis Reveal and the first
  graduation. Founding member agreement includes an optional marketing-rights consent
  (separate checkbox, never bundled, revocable).
- **Honesty rule:** no fabricated, placeholder, or "as seen on" testimonials, ever. Until
  real ones exist, the landing page shows the Founding Grove invite in that slot, and
  Maya's demo grove stays labeled fictional. The first real testimonial replacing the
  invite block is itself a build-in-public post.
- Formats beyond the band: a /customers case-study page per founder story; pull-quotes in
  drip emails; testimonial frames in Herald's short-form clips (with per-use consent).

## 5. Growth loops & metrics

**Loops:** diagnosis share card → visitor → free tier → own diagnosis (the flagship loop);
graduation cards → social proof; referral gifting (give a month, get a month / +1 Nibbin
slot); optional "drafted with Nibbin" footer (off by default, user choice — never forced);
the annual benchmark report → press + SEO.

**Metrics (extends SPEC §6.12):** waitlist size & weekly growth; activation = TTFAD <10 min;
free→paid conversion (target 5–8%); W4 graduate rate (the retention proxy); referral
K-factor (target >0.3 at launch); channel CAC vs $19/$49 LTV (target LTV:CAC >4); case-study
count. Archivist renders this weekly; vanity metrics (impressions, followers) tracked but
never optimized for.

## 6. The Marketing Grove — running GTM as a one-man company

The marketing department is a grove of internal agents. Where the product can do the job,
dogfood Nibbin itself; where it can't yet (video assembly, ad ops), run Claude-based
pipelines that graduate into product connectors later. **The same principle applies: every
agent starts at Student (drafting for approval); John sets the action level for each — promotion to Senior grade (auto-publish routine items) informs the grant, just like the product.** The meta-story — "Nibbin is run
by Nibbins" — is itself Phase 0/1 content.

| Agent | Job | Tools | School rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scribe** | Long-form: SEO articles, newsletter, build-in-public posts from changelogs/gate reports | repo, CMS, email platform | Student forever on thought-leadership voice; Senior allowed on changelog digests |
| **Herald** | Short-form social: clips from creature engine renders, captions, scheduling, comment-reply drafts | engine, video templates, Buffer/Typefully-class scheduler APIs | Senior may schedule pre-approved formats; all replies drafted, never auto-sent |
| **Scout** | Listening: monitors FB groups/Reddit/X/HN for pain threads + brand mentions; drafts genuinely helpful responses; flags urgent (complaints, security mentions) | platform read APIs/RSS, alerting | Read + draft only, permanently — community posts ship from John's hands, always |
| **Quill** | Partnerships/outreach: sources educators & podcasts, personalizes pitches, runs follow-up sequences, tracks affiliate pipeline | email, CRM-lite (sheet/db), enrichment | Velocity-capped exactly like product users (we obey our own anti-spam law); Senior after 50 approved sends |
| **Archivist** | Analytics: weekly growth Field Note — funnel, CAC by channel, cohort retention, experiment readouts | product events DB, Stripe, channel APIs | Senior immediately (read-only by nature) |
| **Concierge** | Support + lifecycle: inbox triage, onboarding nudge drafts, churn-save drafts, FAQ updates from ticket patterns | support inbox, product DB (via admin console grants) | Student on anything customer-facing until 95%/25 like everyone else |

**Hard rules for the Marketing Grove:**
1. **John's voice is non-delegable** where trust is being built: community posts, podcast
   appearances, founder content. Agents draft; the human relationship is the product.
2. **Platform ToS are invariants:** no engagement automation where prohibited, no scraping,
   no fake accounts, rate limits respected. The OAuth-app-killer logic (SPEC §6.9) applies
   to our own outreach.
3. **Disclosure with pride, not fine print:** content made by the grove says so — it's the
   demo. "This clip was assembled by Herald, my social Nibbin" converts better than hiding it.
4. **Approval queue ≤20 min/day:** if the daily queue exceeds that, agents are being too
   chatty — tune them down, don't rubber-stamp.

**Build order:** Scout + Scribe now (Phase 0 needs listening and content); Herald at M5
(needs the engine's render pipeline); Quill at Phase 1; Concierge at beta; Archivist when
events flow (M4+). Each is a Claude Code project using this repo's skills (brand-voice,
design-system) — the marketing grove inherits the same constitution.

## 7. Operating cadence (the one-man week)

- **Daily (20 min):** approval queue — Scout drafts, Herald clips, Concierge replies.
- **Mon (60 min):** content batch — approve/edit the week's videos + article with Scribe.
- **Wed (30 min):** partnerships — Quill's pipeline review, 2 personal touches.
- **Fri (30 min):** Archivist's growth Field Note — kill/double experiments, log decisions here.
- **Monthly:** one experiment ships, one dies. Channel budget rebalanced by CAC.

Budget through Phase 1: $500–1,500/mo (scheduler + video tooling + email platform +
enrichment + small search test). Affiliates are CAC paid on results — turn them up, not ads.
